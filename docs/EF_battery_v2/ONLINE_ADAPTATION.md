# EF battery v2.0: online adaptation for GALS

**Status:** final v1.0, approved 2026-10-22 (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN §13 Q16). Implemented on branch `course-prompting-ef`; scoring version `efa-1.0.0`.

The paper battery (`source/`) is administered in person by a facilitator. GALS gives the same stimuli (`source/stimuli.json`, sha256 `a705e667…`) online and self-administered. **The constructs are the same, but the scores are not equivalent to the paper version.** Presentation, response mode and timing all differ. The pilot and any norms must use the online version.

This document lists:

- every deviation from the facilitator scripts and the scoring key;
- the **exact student-facing wording** for review.

Text in "quotes" is what students see. **Bold** marks a change from the paper script.

Decisions already made (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN §13):

- **Q1:** spoken stimuli are shown on screen.
- **Q2:** the questionnaire comes before the puzzles.
- **Q3:** W5 is run without cloze norming and flagged.
- **Q7:** completing the assessment is required.
- **Q9:** "longest length passed" for W2 means the longest length with at least one correct trial.

## 0. Administration (all tasks)

| Paper (facilitator)                                                                                                                | Online (GALS)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Facilitator reads the scripts, gives practice feedback, times by stopwatch or audio                                                | Instructions on screen; practice feedback generated by the system; timing in the browser with `performance.now()`, onsets aligned to `requestAnimationFrame`                                                                                                                       |
| Participant/facilitator separation: the participant never sees the W1/W2 sequences, W5 sentences or fluency letters (CHANGELOG #1) | **Shown on screen.** This is the main modality change (decision Q1). Stimuli are sent to the browser one trial at a time; answer keys never leave the server                                                                                                                       |
| Order: C1–C7 → W1–W7 → C8–C9; Background (A) and ADEXI (B) **after** the puzzles at T0                                             | **Questionnaire before the puzzles** (decision Q2): at T0, A + B + C1–C7 come before the puzzles, then W1–W7, then C8–C9. The state check C1–C7 stays closest to the puzzles                                                                                                       |
| Administration log filled in by the facilitator                                                                                    | Filled in automatically: device, browser, screen and window size, full screen accepted, sensing paused, start/end, minutes since the last course activity, focus-loss totals                                                                                                       |
| Room noise / lighting / mode recorded                                                                                              | **Not recorded.** The notice asks for a quiet place; C9 (disruptions) is the proxy                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Breaks as needed                                                                                                                   | Optional untimed break screen between puzzles                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Paper booklet: "Please do not turn a page until the facilitator asks you to"                                                       | One puzzle per full-screen view; the next view opens only after the previous one finishes                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Interruptions handled by the facilitator                                                                                           | A puzzle reloaded or closed mid-way is marked `interrupted` and **never re-run** (its items would be exposed); the student continues with the next puzzle. Staff can reset one puzzle with a reason, which re-shows the same items and is flagged `items_reexposed` (decision Q13) |
| Course pop-ups n/a on paper                                                                                                        | The course's periodic "How are you feeling right now?" pop-up is **held back** while the puzzles page is open and appears after the student leaves it                                                                                                                              |
| Sensing (camera, eye tracking) n/a on paper                                                                                        | Eye tracking, pupil measurement, webcam recording and replay snapshots are **off** during the questionnaire and puzzles. Focus and visibility of the tab are still logged                                                                                                          |

**Progress text:** "Puzzle _n_ of _N_", with no task names.
**Break screen:** "Take a short break if you like. Continue when you are ready."

## 1. Questionnaire ("About you and how you feel today")

- The wording is **verbatim** from `questionnaires.tex` / `ef.py`; this is enforced by tests.
- **Deviation:** the ADEXI instructions keep the paper wording "Please **circle** a number…" unchanged, because the items must be reproduced unchanged. Online, the student clicks a numbered button that shows its label ("1 · Definitely not true" … "5 · Definitely true"). Please confirm that keeping "circle" is acceptable, or provide approved online wording.
- Part A items are typed or selected; "Other:" and "If yes, roughly when?" are optional free text.
- **A13** (diagnosis) appears only if `efShowDiagnosisItem` is on, which needs ethics approval.

---

## 2. Colour check (before W6)

Paper: "Please say the colour of each patch." The participant names three patches aloud.
Online: "What colour is each patch? Click the name under each one."

- Patch order is randomised; labels are fixed (red, green, blue).
- No feedback is given.
- Any wrong answer silently skips W6 and records "colour check failed".

## 3. Tasks: paper → online, with on-screen wording

### W1 Number repetition (`dsf`/`dsb`)

|              | Paper                                                               | Online                                                                                               |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Presentation | Facilitator reads 1 digit/s                                         | **Digits one at a time in the centre: 800 ms on, 200 ms blank** (1 digit/s, as on paper)             |
| Response     | Oral                                                                | **Typed:** digits only, backspace allowed, Enter to submit                                           |
| Practice     | 1 trial per part; if wrong, give the correct answer and repeat once | Same: feedback "Correct" / "Not quite — the answer was 9 3. Let's try that one again." Repeated once |
| Discontinue  | Both trials at a length failed                                      | Same (computed on the server)                                                                        |
| Scores       | Forward 0–16, backward 0–14, longest span                           | Same                                                                                                 |

On-screen wording:

- Forward: "Some numbers will appear one at a time. When they have finished, type them in the same order, then press Enter."
- Backward: "Now type the numbers **backwards**. For example, if you see 7 then 1, type 1 7."

### W2 Sorting numbers and letters (`lns`)

|              | Paper                                                | Online                                                   |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Presentation | Oral, 1 item/s                                       | **Visual, 800 ms on / 200 ms blank**                     |
| Response     | Oral                                                 | **Typed;** case and separators ignored                   |
| Practice     | 2 items, feedback + correct answer                   | Same                                                     |
| Scores       | 0–12; "longest length passed" (undefined in the key) | 0–12; longest length with ≥1 correct trial (decision Q9) |

On-screen wording: "A mix of numbers and letters will appear one at a time. When they have finished, type the numbers first, from smallest to largest, and then the letters in alphabetical order. For example, if you see 9 C 3, type 3 9 C."

### W3 Adding and subtracting (`pm`)

|              | Paper                                                             | Online                                                                                  |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Presentation | Booklet, 3 columns × 10 per list                                  | **One number at a time**; each list (30 items) on its own screen                        |
| Response     | Written in boxes                                                  | **Number box + Enter;** the next item appears immediately                               |
| Timing       | Stopwatch from "Begin" to the last answer written                 | First item onset → last Enter, per list; **plus per-item RT** (new)                     |
| List 3 cue   | Booklet: "Add 3 to the first number, subtract 3 from the second…" | Same instruction above the number, with **no per-item cue** for which operation applies |
| Errors       | Wrong, skipped or illegible                                       | Wrong (no skipping possible; Enter with an empty box is ignored)                        |
| Scores       | Times, errors, switch cost T3 − ½(T1+T2), >3-error flag           | Same                                                                                    |

On-screen wording:

- List 1: "Add 3 to each number."
- List 2: "Subtract 3 from each number."
- List 3: "Add 3 to the first number, subtract 3 from the second, add 3 to the third, and so on."
- Each list also says: "Type each answer and press Enter. Work as quickly as you can without making mistakes."
- Practice (`pm.practice`) has feedback.

### W4 Word lists (`flu`)

|                     | Paper                                           | Online                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Response            | Spoken; facilitator writes; audio optional      | **Typed:** one word per entry, Enter adds it to a visible list; earlier entries can't be edited                                                                                  |
| Time                | 60 s per prompt from the end of the instruction | 60 s from the first display of the prompt, with a visible countdown; the prompt stays visible                                                                                    |
| "Keep going" prompt | Said once if the participant stops before 30 s  | **Shown once** ("Keep going! Any other words?") if there is no entry for 15 s before the 30 s mark                                                                               |
| Scoring             | Two blind raters from the transcript            | Raw entries with timestamps; **automatic pre-scoring** (repeats, same-root variants, wrong initial letter, likely names); **final scoring by two blind raters** in the coding UI |

On-screen wording:

- Letters: "Type as many words as you can that begin with the letter shown, as quickly as you can. Press Enter after each word. For example, for the letter N you might type nail, night, near. Do not use names of people or places, like Nancy or Nepal, and do not use numbers. Also, do not type the same word again with a different ending, like eat and eating. You will have one minute."
- Animals: "Now type as many different animals as you can. You have one minute."
- Switching: "Now switch back and forth between two kinds of things: one of the first kind, then one of the second kind, and so on. For example, if the kinds were colours and body parts, you would type red, arm, blue, leg. You have one minute."
- **Typing speed** now limits output, unlike speech; counts are not comparable with spoken norms.

### W5 Finishing sentences (`hay`)

|              | Paper                                                                 | Online                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Presentation | Read aloud, natural pace                                              | **Whole sentence shown as text**, gap at the end                                                                                                                        |
| Response     | One spoken word                                                       | **One typed word,** Enter to submit                                                                                                                                     |
| RT           | From the end of the facilitator's last word to response onset (audio) | **From sentence onset to the first keystroke, and to submit.** Sentence length (characters) is recorded, so reading time can be modelled                                |
| Prompt       | "Any word" after 15 s, tick P                                         | Same: "Any word" appears after 15 s with no input; prompted items are kept and flagged                                                                                  |
| Practice     | 2 per part with feedback                                              | Same; Part A feedback shows a sensible word; Part B shows an example unconnected word                                                                                   |
| Coding       | Facilitator codes Part B A/B/0; blind second scoring                  | **Two blind raters** in the coding UI, with the `HAYLING_CODING_EXAMPLES` anchors; code A pre-filled where the response equals the expected word, and must be confirmed |
| Norming      | Cloze norming (≥80%, n ≥ 30) required before use                      | **Not done** (decision Q3); exports carry `w5_unnormed`                                                                                                                 |

On-screen wording:

- Part A: "Each sentence has its last word missing. As soon as you see it, type one word that completes the sentence so that it makes good sense, then press Enter. Be as quick as you can."
- Part B: "Now type a word that does **not** fit the sentence in any way: a word that has nothing to do with the sentence and makes it nonsense. For example, for 'The rabbit hopped across the …', you might type 'umbrella'. Be as quick as you can."

### W6 Colours (`stroop`)

|          | Paper                                                  | Online                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Format   | Golden card: 5 × 20 grid, 45 s per page, oral naming   | **Same grid** (reading order down the columns), **keyboard responses:** F = red, J = green, K = blue, with labels on screen; the current item is highlighted |
| Practice | Practice row per page                                  | Practice row (`stroop.practice`, with feedback) **plus a key-mapping block of about 12 items** (new, to learn the keys)                                      |
| Errors   | Facilitator says "No"; participant corrects; time runs | **The item flashes red, keys are ignored for 400 ms, and the correct key must be pressed; time keeps running** (see §6)                                      |
| Restart  | Restart from the top if finished                       | Same                                                                                                                                                         |
| Scores   | W, C, CW items completed; Golden IG = CW − (W×C)/(W+C) | Same; **plus per-item RT** and corrected-error counts                                                                                                        |

On-screen wording:

- W page: "Press the key for each **word**, going down each column."
- C page: "Press the key for the **colour of the ink** of each item, going down each column."
- CW page: "Press the key for the colour of the **INK**, not the word, going down each column."
- All pages: "F = red, J = green, K = blue. If you make a mistake, the item flashes red. Press the right key and carry on. You have 45 seconds."
- **Key-press naming** replaces speech; the size of the interference effect is expected to differ from oral norms.

### W7 Joining circles (`tmtA`/`tmtB`)

|             | Paper                                                                               | Online                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Format      | Pen on paper, 150 × 185 mm field                                                    | **Click trail** on a fixed-aspect canvas scaled from mm, with the circle diameter scaled to match; the scale (px per mm) is recorded; y-axis flipped (the source is y-up) |
| Errors      | Facilitator points to the error; pen returns to the last correct circle; clock runs | **Wrong click flashes red and counts as an error;** continue from the last correct circle; clock runs                                                                     |
| Line        | Drawn by the participant                                                            | Drawn by the system on each correct click                                                                                                                                 |
| Discontinue | 300 s (record 300 and circles reached)                                              | Same                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Practice    | Sample trails                                                                       | Same (`meta.tmt_samples`)                                                                                                                                                 |

On-screen wording:

- Practice 1: "Click the circles in order: 1, 2, 3, and so on."
- Test 1: "Click the numbers in order: 1, 2, 3, … up to 25, as quickly as you can."
- Practice 2: "Click the circles in order, switching between numbers and letters: 1, A, 2, B, 3, C, …"
- Test 2: "Switch between numbers and letters in order: 1, A, 2, B, 3, C, … up to 13, as quickly as you can."
- **Mouse/trackpad clicking** replaces drawing; times are not comparable with pen-and-paper norms.

## 4. Scoring and data

- Scoring rules follow `scoring_key.tex`; the computed scores carry `scoringVersion` (`efa-1.0.0`) and can be recomputed from the raw trials.
- New raw data not on paper:
  - per-item RTs (W3, W6);
  - first-keystroke RTs (W5);
  - per-entry timestamps (W4);
  - per-click timestamps (W7);
  - on-screen durations (W1/W2);
  - focus loss per trial.
- No z-scores or composites in the app; these are computed at analysis against the T0 sample (`ef_report.tex`).
- Data-quality flags:
  - colour check failed (W6 removed);
  - interrupted;
  - focus lost;
  - discontinued (W7 at 300 s);
  - more than 3 errors (W3);
  - prompted (W5);
  - `w5_unnormed`;
  - `items_reexposed`.

### 4.1 Scoring as implemented (`apps/api/src/efa/scoring/scoring.ts`)

| Task  | Scores (per run)                                                                                                                                                        | Notes                                                                            |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| W1    | fwd/bwd total correct (0–16 / 0–14), longest span, trials given                                                                                                         | Discontinue after both trials of a length fail                                   |
| W2    | total correct, longest length with ≥1 correct trial (Q9), trials given                                                                                                  | Same discontinue rule                                                            |
| W3    | time per list (s), errors per list, mean item RT, **switch cost = T3 − ½(T1+T2)**, switch ratio                                                                         | An unfinished list has no time; missing items count as errors; >3 errors flagged |
| W4    | entries and automatic valid count per prompt; after coding: valid, repeats, rule breaks; switching: **correct switches**, same-category pairs, intrusions, switch ratio | Final scores wait for two raters (`awaiting_coding`)                             |
| W5    | mean first-key RT for A and B, B − A; after coding: **B error score = 3 × A + 1 × B** (0–45), A and B counts; prompted count                                            | Flag `w5_unnormed` (Q3)                                                          |
| W6    | items completed W/C/CW (correct presses in 45 s), corrected errors, **Golden IG = CW − (W×C)/(W+C)**, CW/C, mean per-item RT, RT interference                           | Skipped when the colour check fails                                              |
| W7    | time (s), errors and circles reached for Parts 1 and 2                                                                                                                  | Part 2 stopped at 300 s (time scored 300, flagged)                               |
| ADEXI | WM (9–45), INH (5–25), 12-item variants without items 1 and 14                                                                                                          | Computed into the questionnaire export                                           |

Scores are written when a puzzle finishes or is interrupted and can be recomputed for a new version without touching raw data.

### 4.2 Rater coding (W4, W5)

Two blind raters per entry (no student or occasion shown; random order per rater). W4 codes: valid / repeat / rule break / intrusion, plus the category of each valid word when switching; the automatic pre-scoring (repeat, same root, wrong letter, likely name) is shown as a hint only. W5 Part B codes: A (fits the sentence) / B (related or indirect) / 0 (unrelated), with the expected word and the `HAYLING_CODING_EXAMPLES` anchors; **A is pre-filled when the response is exactly the expected word and is confirmed by saving**. Agreeing codes become final; disagreements are adjudicated by the course owner. Cohen's κ per task is reported on the coding page and in `ef_coding.csv`.

### 4.3 Timing check

In a full browser run (Chromium, 60 Hz display), every W1/W2 item was measured on screen for 800 ms (26 of 26; target 800 ± 20 ms). Each item's measured on-screen time is stored (`shown_ms`), so the check can be repeated on real data.

## 5. Decisions on the open points (2026-10-22, Q16)

1. ADEXI "circle a number" wording: **kept verbatim** (students click a number).
2. W4 keep-going nudge: **after 15 s without an entry, before the 30 s mark.**
3. W5 RT: **from sentence onset**, with sentence length recorded for modelling.
4. W6 keys: **F = red, J = green, K = blue**, shown on screen.
5. Practice feedback wording: **approved as drafted.**

## 6. Changes after the validity audit (2026-11-13, scoring version `efa-1.1.0`)

An audit of the implementation against `scoring_key.tex` found the formulas correct and the timing sound (`performance.now()`, onsets after paint), and these issues, now fixed:

1. **W6 key-guessing.** With no cost for a wrong key, cycling F → J → K passed any item in about two presses without reading it, so CW (and IG) could exceed honest performance. A wrong key now locks the keys for **400 ms**, playing the role of the time a spoken correction costs on the paper version (Golden, 1978). Held-key auto-repeat is ignored. A page with more than 25 % wrong presses is flagged `<page>_high_error_rate` in the task-score flags.
2. **Server-side time limit (W6).** Presses later than 45 s (+250 ms tolerance) after the page onset are not counted; the number dropped is flagged `<page>_late_presses_dropped`.
3. **Parts never given are missing, not zero.** In an interrupted run, W1 backward, W2, a W3 list, or a W6 page that was never presented now scores `null` instead of 0 (or "30 errors"); Golden IG and the W3 switch cost are `null` when one of their parts is missing. `run_status` stays in the export.
4. **W4 unsubmitted word.** A word still in the box when the 60 s ends is stored as `unsubmitted` in the trial response. It is not scored.
5. **No 24 h expiry.** An occasion idle for more than 24 h no longer locks: the student continues, and the occasion is flagged `resumedAfterIdle`. Pre-register this flag as an exclusion criterion or covariate, since the before-questions (C1–C7) may be stale for puzzles done days later.

Open points (not changed):

- W5 Part A errors are not rater-coded, although `scoring_key.tex` records them. Either add Part A to the coding queue or report the deviation.
- The ADEXI "12-item variant" in `scoring.ts` could not be traced to a publication. Check the source before reporting it.
- Hayling stems have no local cloze norms (decision Q3), so Part B may sit at floor. Check the Part B distribution after the pilot.
- W3 switch cost is a difference score from one list per condition, so expect low reliability (Hedge, Powell & Sumner, 2018).
